POSC 8410: Confounders vs. Colliders

Post-midterm review

Will Horne

Confounder or Collider?

  • Confounder: condition on it. Leave it out and the \(X\)–\(Y\) association is spurious.
  • Collider: do not condition on it. Condition on it and you create a spurious association.
  • Conditioning includes controlling for it and keeping only some of its values in your sample. “Only published studies” and “only winners” on the midterm were both colliders.

Do Waffle Houses Cause Divorce?

  • States with more Waffle Houses have more divorce: \(r =\) 0.37

  • Waffle House is a Southern chain. Being in the South and Waffle House density correlate at 0.76, and Southern states divorce more (10.7 vs. 9.3 per 1,000)

  • Control for age at time of first marriage and the relationship goes away.

Data: McElreath, Statistical Rethinking, ch. 5. 50 states, 2009 divorce rates.

Slope per extra Waffle House per million: 0.074 (p = 0.009) with no controls; 0.050 (SE 0.042, p = 0.24) controlling for South. It shrinks but does not reach zero, and the SE grows because South and Waffle Houses are so tightly linked. A yes/no South indicator measures “Southern-ness” crudely. Outside the South there is still a small positive slope (0.42, p = 0.04), driven by border states such as OK, KS, and IN that have a few Waffle Houses and above-average divorce. That is leftover regional confounding, not a Waffle House effect. In McElreath’s DAG the South works through age at marriage and marriage rate.

Collider: Do Police Use More Force on Black Civilians?

  • Police records include only people who were stopped, so the data are conditioned on a collider before any researcher touches them.
  • Race and suspicion both cause stops (arrows point into Stopped). If officers stop Black civilians on thinner grounds, those stopped are on average less suspicious.
  • So comparing force by race within the records compares unlike groups, which can understate bias or hide it entirely (Knox, Lowe & Mummolo 2020, APSR).

Your Turn: Confounder or Collider?

For each, name the third variable and say whether you should condition on it.

1. Using newspaper reports, a researcher finds that small protests turn violent more often than large ones. Newspapers cover a protest if it is large or if it is violent.

Collider: coverage. Size and violence both cause it. A dataset built from news reports keeps only covered protests, so a small protest had to be violent to get in.

2. A columnist notes that countries with more McDonald’s restaurants per person go to war less often.

Confounder: economic development. It produces both the restaurants and peace. Compare countries at similar levels of development.

3. Congresswomen secure about 9% more federal spending for their districts than congressmen (Anzia & Berry 2011, AJPS). Thus, women are better at gaining funds for their districts than men (?)

Collider: winning office. If women face higher barriers to winning, only the most skilled women win, so gender and skill are linked among winners. That is selection, not an effect of being a woman.
